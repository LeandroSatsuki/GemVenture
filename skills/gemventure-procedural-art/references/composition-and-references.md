# Composition and reference record

Research reviewed 2026-10-12. These are references, not alternate project instructions or assets licensed for copying.

- [Forager — developer](https://www.hopfrogsa.net/forager): inspect published moving examples for large masses, readable actors and simple resource silhouettes. Adopt the clarity, retain Gemventure's palette and perspective.
- [Stardew Valley — official media](https://www.stardewvalley.net/media/) and [press](https://www.stardewvalley.net/press/): inspect authored space, separated walkable paths and terrain boundaries. Mine layout inspiration means varied rooms/corridors and deliberate furnishing, not reproducing sprites or assuming its generation algorithm.
- [Crypt of the NecroDancer — official press](https://braceyourselfgames.com/press/crypt-of-the-necrodancer/): read dangers from tile geometry and distinct silhouettes.
- [Ryan Clark: Finding the beat in Crypt of the NecroDancer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/game-design-deep-dive-finding-the-beat-in-i-crypt-of-the-necrodancer-i-): fair readable outcomes and visually unified musical actions are useful design principles. Preserve Gemventure's existing timing unless a timing change is requested.
- [Jeremy Price: Elements and Principles of Design in Level Art](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/applying-the-elements-of-design-and-principles-of-design-in-level-art): prioritize readable focal points, repetition and unity. Limit background detail when combat already carries motion and effects.
- [Two-step Constructive Approaches for Dungeon Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04660): separate architectural generation from placement of starts, goals, challenges and rewards; validate both stages.

## Applied hierarchy

Player/attack telegraph > enemy/interactive crystal > mineable rock > wall edge > floor decoration. A strong effect may bloom briefly, but it cannot permanently obscure attack warnings or swallow input. Use size and motion as well as color, so reward and threat are distinguishable without reading.

Village: distribute stations around an open shared space with clear paths and room to expand. Animate a few meaningful parts at low amplitude rather than bobbing whole buildings. Keep solid foundations still. The village and camps are rest spaces: gentle ambient motion and soft music.

Caves: create connected chamber clusters, loops and broad corridors with characteristic biome terrain. Avoid long one-cell pinches in a rhythm game with swarms. Keep permanent cliff faces connected and lower-contrast than collectible rocks. Decoration grows in small edge clusters; valuable crystals occupy legible pockets. Maintain a clear boss arena even when the surrounding cave changes.

## Visual acceptance questions

- Can the silhouette be named without its label at actual gameplay size?
- Do shadow shapes explain a surface instead of adding arbitrary facets?
- Can a player distinguish wall, breakable rock, crystal and creature during an explosion?
- Does the same family remain recognizable across seeds and biomes?
- Are roofs, stars, foundations and gem clusters structurally coherent?
- Are moving feet grounded, accents attached to their pivots and reduced motion respected?
- Does procedural variety change the route, not merely scatter more noise?
